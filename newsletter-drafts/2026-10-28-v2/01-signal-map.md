## One underrated idea (single newsletter)

Cannonball GTM keeps a "failed list": every pain that fails one of the four questions is written down with the question it failed, so the bar is visible. Almost nobody keeps a record of what they rejected.

## One contradiction

GTM Engineering and Clay advertising push breadth: more datapoints, more enrichment, "find unique data" (Clay's own mail). Cannonball pushes the opposite: one "no" and a pain is off the map. Both claim to improve targeting. The article takes Cannonball's side and adds a time test.

## Three candidate angles

1. **Every buying signal has a clock** (Cannonball, GTM Engineering, GTMnow, GTM Strategist, The Signal). Payoff: a founder audits signals by window against reply time and moves money to the shortest window. **Chosen.**
2. **Brand is the hidden conversion engine inside outbound** (Datarails, Cannonball, Growth Unhinged, GTM Strategist). Payoff: a founder gates outbound by prior exposure. Rejected, see stress test.
3. **Hire a GTM engineer at Series B and make RevOps the system of record** (The Signal, GTM Engineering, GTMnow). Payoff: a founder splits one role into two. Rejected, see stress test.

## Stress test (sceptical GTM operator)

Is the chosen angle obvious? "Speed to lead matters" is a decade old. What none of the sources says is that **signals should be priced by window minus reply time**, and that most signal lists have no window column at all. Does everyone already say it? The sources sell the signal (Clay promotion, Cannonball's kit, Braze). Nobody tells a founder to cut signals. Would a founder change a decision? Yes: audit own reply time this week, rank signals by window, move spend to the shortest window and pause the weakest. Weak spot: the "five times" rule is my own rule of thumb, flagged as such in the article.

### The two strongest rejected angles

**A. Brand as the hidden engine of outbound** (gate outbound sends by prior exposure). It lost because it rests on one anecdote ("I see your ads all the time", per GTMnow) and Datarails' own admission that four attribution methods often disagree. A founder could not act on it without data nobody can produce this quarter, and "brand matters" is the loudest message in the label already. It also edges toward last week's rejected "land in brand, expand in growth" territory.

**B. The GTM engineer is the new RevOps hire.** It lost because it is org design advice, the authors with the strongest material (Alex Lindahl, The Signal's author) work for or are sponsored by vendors that sell to GTM engineers, and "hire this role" is a generic process decision with no point of view none of the sources already states.

## Chosen angle and why

Angle 1. It sits in five newsletters, converts a vague mood ("signals matter") into a decision (what to pause, who owns the first response, how fast), and adds a point of view none of them states: **a signal is worth its window less your reply time**, so many signals you pay for are worth nothing.
