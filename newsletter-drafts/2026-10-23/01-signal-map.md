## One underrated idea (single newsletter)

GTM Weekly: Wispr pays creators 7% of ad spend, uncapped, every 7 days, because a software deal takes weeks and the DTC pay-per-sale model cannot wait. The payout clock was reset to match the sales clock. Nobody else discusses how the *payment terms* shape a loop.

## One contradiction

GTM Strategist says to "shock the system" with something radically different (Datarails' telenovela). GTM Weekly says that for paid creative, diversity beats genius: many different ads, not one brilliant one. Both are right, in different currencies: attention for launches, algorithmic distinctness for paid.

## Three candidate angles

1. **Every growth loop pays someone in some currency; choose currency, trigger, clock and ceiling on purpose.** Payoff: a founder can fix creator, referral, invite or rep pay terms this week.
2. **Launch runways beat launch days.** Payoff: a six month plan. (Weak: GTM Strategist already says it in full, and it is on every feed.)
3. **RevOps and GTM engineering need separate swim lanes.** Payoff: an org chart. (Narrow, and The Signal owns it.)

## Chosen angle

Angle 1. It joins three unrelated sources (Instinct's invites, Wispr's creator payouts, the pay difference between a RevOps and a GTM engineering role) that each describe a payment but none of which names the design decision. It also tells a founder to do something with a contract, not just agree with a theory.

## Stress test (sceptical GTM operator)

- *"Incentive design is obvious."* Everyone nods at "you get what you pay for"; very few write down the trigger event, the payout interval and whether the payee can inflate the trigger. The article's new claim is that a trigger the payer controls is safer than one the payee reports, with Wispr's spend payout as the worked case.
- *"Is this just a creator programme tip?"* No: the same four decisions explain Instinct's invites (the currency is access) and a rep's variable pay (the trigger is meetings booked).
- *"Would a founder change a decision?"* Yes: rewrite the payout terms of a referral scheme, creator brief, agency retainer or GTM engineer's plan, and stop paying on events the payee can cheaply produce.
- *"Evidence is thin."* True: Wispr's terms are second hand (Ira Bodnar on X, via Work-Bench) and Instinct's figures are reported, not audited. The article flags this and presents the model as a way to ask questions, not as proven law.
- Verdict: survives. The draft must lead with the choice and concede early that the examples are reported, not audited.
