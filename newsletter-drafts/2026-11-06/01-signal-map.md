## Three candidate angles
A. **Rent your channels, own the list.** Channels are leased and interchangeable; the audience list that moves between them is the asset, and nobody owns it. Payoff: a founder can name a list owner and run a one number test (days to move a third of spend) this week.
B. **Systems before headcount.** Hire the GTM engineer or fix speed to lead before the next AE (Bessemer, Cram, Green). Payoff: a hiring decision for Q1.
C. **Your next top of funnel is a buyer's agent.** Make the site machine readable (Encore index, Adobe AI traffic, GrowthHackers' "three ways" item). Payoff: a website to do list.

## Pick: A
It is the only angle that explains several of the changes at once (channel churn, conversion happening off site, smaller teams, signal based buying) and forces a Q1 budget decision. It also adds a view none of the sources states: that the audience list is the one go to market asset that compounds, so it needs a single owner.

## Stress test (sceptical GTM operator)
- *Obvious?* "Know your ICP" is obvious. The claim here is narrower: five channels each hold their own list, and the cost shows up as a $500 lead with a 10 to 20% fit rate (per Growth Unhinged, citing Primer). Survives.
- *Everywhere already?* Primer and ABM writers say "separate audience from media". Nobody says who should own it or proposes the switching test. Partly survives; the article credits Primer openly.
- *Would a founder change a decision?* Yes: name an owner, cut one channel line to fund list work, and fix the budget structure before Q1 plans lock.

### Two strongest rejected angles
**B. Systems before headcount.** Strongest because it has three sources and a clear Q1 decision. Rejected because (1) the GTM engineer hiring story is the most saturated topic in this label, and a sceptical founder has read it ten times; (2) the evidence is one VC survey summary, one recruiter anecdote and one SDR-services founder with no data (Green, whose audience sells managed SDRs); (3) it does not describe a change in how startups reach buyers, only in who sits in the seat. It is folded in as one section ("Why the decision cannot wait").
**C. The buyer's agent as top of funnel.** Strongest on novelty. Rejected because the main dataset is from a vendor that sells the score (Encore, 2,055 organisations; single source), only 6% are reported as connectable, and no newsletter shows a startup that changed pipeline by acting on it. A founder cannot make a Q1 decision on it with confidence. Also close to the 28 Oct v2 lead source territory (agents as buyers, Exa).

Also considered and dropped: "AI adoption comes in waves by verification speed" (The Signal), because it is a framing, not a decision.
