## Themes in three or more newsletters
1. **AI agents are becoming a second audience for GTM surfaces (websites, directories, chat), but the evidence of what agents can actually do is thin and mixed.** The GTM Engineering Newsletter covers this twice: the "AI harness" rebranding wave and G2's new agent evaluations (18 Nov), and the flight-booking task-completion test plus 8 MCP distribution routes (12 Nov). GTM Strategist's website issue assumes the same shift (building pages for agents as well as humans). The GTM Engineer Pulse carries Ross Simmonds' note that Reddit's falling search referrals mean "your blog probably is too," and that founders should check how often AI answers name them.
2. **The buying window is narrow and most of a list is not in it right now, so the newest advice is smaller, more specific, more timely targeting rather than broader reach.** The Signal's whole issue (micro-campaigns, the Signal-Based Market), Work-Bench's GTM Weekly (95% of the market is not buying; plant the idea early), and MRR Unlocked's dashboard piece (tier contacts by ICP fit before counting them, or a few good-fit SQLs get buried in volume).
3. **GTM headcount is shifting towards fewer, broader roles plus automation, and the boundary between them is unsettled.** The GTM Engineer Pulse is built around this (companies hiring a second GTM engineer and splitting the job by motion, layer or level; Demodesk dropping job titles). GTMnow's profile of Sierra, Legora and Exa shows the same shift on the post-sale side (2 post-sale hires for every 1 pre-sale hire at Legora, forward-deployed engineers at Sierra). The GTM Engineering Newsletter's harness piece frames the same question as who owns "the context layer."

## One underrated idea from a single newsletter
MRR Unlocked's smallest recommendation is also its most portable: alongside whatever your analytics report, keep one open-text CRM field, "how did you hear about us," in the prospect's own words. Their point is that analytics might say "Direct Traffic" while the buyer says "I've followed the founder on LinkedIn for months." It costs nothing and it is the cheapest way to see word of mouth and AI-assisted discovery that no dashboard tags correctly yet.

## One contradiction
The GTM Engineering Newsletter runs both sides of the same argument two editions apart, from the same author. On 12 November it reports agent web traffic overtaking human traffic and GTM teams racing to win MCP distribution. Its own cited evidence, a 20-assistant flight-booking test by AI Search Leaders, shows agents with their own browser completing 21 of 30 runs and chat-only agents completing only 2 of 24, meaning most of the "agent audience" cannot yet finish a real task unassisted. On 18 November, the same newsletter covers HubSpot's rebrand to "the Customer Harness Company," then reports that G2's first agent evaluation scored HubSpot's own Agent Hub at 95 out of 250, behind category leaders Tidio and Retell AI at 200. The publication pushing hardest on "build for the harness" is also the one showing the leading harness brand underperforming on the test built to grade harnesses.

## Three candidate angles
A. **Build for the agent that already shows up, not the one you fear missing.** A sceptical look at the rush to be "agent ready," using the newsletters' own evidence that most agents still fail the task, that the highest-upside distribution routes cannot be requested and can be withdrawn overnight, and recommending the cheap, durable fixes over a bespoke MCP build. Payoff: a founder gets a short list of what to fix this quarter and what to park.
B. **Micro-campaigns and the Signal-Based Market.** Small, specific, timely lists beat broad reach. Payoff: a one-week test to run a first micro-campaign.
C. **The second GTM hire forces a decision the first one never did.** Role splits (motion, layer, level) once a team adds a second GTM engineer or a post-sale specialist. Payoff: a hiring and role-design decision for Q1.

## Pick: A
It is the only angle that adds a point of view none of the nine newsletters states. Every source covering agents and AI distribution treats "get agent ready" as an unambiguous good; none of them weighs the completion-rate evidence against the build cost, or flags that the best-performing distribution route is also the one you cannot request and can lose overnight. It forces a real decision (what to build this quarter, what to defer) rather than a general mood.

### Stress test (sceptical GTM operator)
- *Obvious?* "AI hype is ahead of AI capability" is a common line generally. The claim here is narrower and sourced: inside the same newsletter and the same week, the data shows most agents cannot finish a real task, and the most visible "harness" brand scored near the bottom of the first test built to grade harnesses. That is not a generic hot take; it is this week's own sources disagreeing with their own framing. Survives.
- *Everywhere already?* Scepticism about AI hype generally, yes. This specific comparison (agent task-completion data versus "agent ready" advice, in the same publication, in the same fortnight) does not appear anywhere in the nine newsletters. Survives.
- *Would a founder change a decision?* Yes: it argues against spending Q1 budget or headcount on a bespoke MCP server or chasing "enhanced distribution," and argues for cheaper, durable fixes (a stable pricing and FAQ structure, tracking citations, the open-text "how did you hear about us" field) that pay off regardless of how fast agents improve.

It is also close to the 6 Nov issue's thesis (owning an audience list versus renting channels) and the 28 Oct v2 issue (buying signals and timing), so running it this week risks repeating ground already covered twice.
**C. The second GTM hire.** Strongest on data (Pulse's whole issue, Timeplot's hiring numbers, three named role splits). Rejected because GTM hiring and the build-versus-buy stack question was the explicit theme of the 12 Nov issue two weeks ago, and this week's evidence (a second hire, a role split) is a narrower restatement of the same territory rather than a new decision.
